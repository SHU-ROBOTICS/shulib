## The two findings that changed the brief mid-flight

Both were found by adversarially reviewing the brief *after* it was committed and while the chunk was
running. Both were checked directly rather than taken from the review.

### 1. Additive growth of a frozen surface currently breaks the build — a D2/D3 defect

`version.hpp` calls an appended enumerator **additive**, requiring a `kApiMinor` bump, and calls that
*"the intended growth path of every frozen surface"*. But both freeze pins hard-assert the current
version:

- `test/f6_signature_pin_test.cpp:349` — `CHECK(shulib::kApiMinor == 0);`
- `test/routine_signature_pin_test.cpp:301` — `CHECK(shulib::kApiMinor == 0);`

D1 §11 had explicitly anticipated that F1 would append a `RoutineStopCause` enumerator. So **the
documented, legal, intended path turns both freeze pins red.** The pins conflate "the version
mechanism exists and the surface is API 2.x" (what D2/D3 meant) with "the version is exactly 2.0"
(what they wrote).

**Rule 4 applies — it is fixed in the pins that own it**, not worked around in F1. The chunk agent
had already hit this and accepted the Rule 4 framing (`F1-PROGRESS.md`, `02:46:19`). **Verify the fix
landed**: `kApiMinor` should be `1` with a pin that no longer forbids additive growth, and the defect
should be recorded in `F1-COMPLETED.md` as belonging to D2/D3.

A pin firing on a **version** line is now an understood event with a known fix. A pin firing on a
**signature or member** line still means stop and report.

### 2. The flagship example is broken in two ways, not one

`chassis.moveTo(p).then(intake.in)` — quoted in the master plan §17 (line 713), the guide, the
cookbook, the roadmap and the generated reference:

- **`intake.in` is not callable** when `in` is an ordinary member function (compiler-verified).
- **`chassis.moveTo(p)` returns `ExitReason`, and an enum has no `.then()`.** `D1-COMPLETED.md` §3
  states this in its first sentence and it was never propagated to §17. Making it compile would mean
  reshaping frozen F6 verbs — rejected at D1, frozen against at D2.

So for every `chassis.`-receiver occurrence the only correct action is to **fix the prose** (the
honest spelling is a `Routine` chain). **No `Chassis` change may be proposed to accommodate it.**

## Process lesson worth keeping: a biased skeptic manufactures a false all-clear

The red-team raised **23 findings and reported 0 survivors.** That verdict was wrong — the two above
were among the "refuted" ones. The cause was in the harness, not the reviewers: the refuting agents
were instructed to *default to refuted*, which is right for suppressing noise and wrong for producing
a trustworthy all-clear.

**The finders earned their keep; the refuters actively hurt.** If this pattern is used again, either
drop the refutation stage or make it argue both sides and report a confidence, never a verdict.

The project already knew the converse — `verify/README.md`: *"a red gate is a question, not a
verdict."* This session established the other half: **a green verdict from a skeptic you biased is
also a question.**
